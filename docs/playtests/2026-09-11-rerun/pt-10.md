# Playtester 10 — mobile, persona: curious explorer

## Summary (5 lines max)
Tower Siege is a merge-and-build tower defense: drag tray pieces onto a gate to build/merge a wall of cannons, survive numbered waves, and spend star coins on a permanent "Blueprints" upgrade shop between runs. The core loop (place pieces, watch cannons fire, survive to the next wave) is graspable within seconds through pure trial and error, but almost every secondary system — merging, VOLLEY, the four separate resource currencies, status effects (fire/frost on blocks), and the entire permanent-progression shop — is discovered only by curious poking, not taught. Both runs ended without ever learning exactly *why* the wall fell (multiple simultaneous effects, no combat log, no per-block health numbers). Progression between runs (blueprints, daily missions, rank/record) is genuinely motivating once found, but a non-curious player would likely miss the blueprint shop's existence entirely for their first several runs.

## Run 1 — what happened (timeline with game-time stamps)
- **~2 s (first impression):** Game loads straight into "WAVE 1·1", a "MONSTERS INCOMING!" banner, a top bar with 3 unlabeled resource counters (100/1/0), a highlighted lane, and a bottom tray of 3 items plus a VOLLEY button. No tutorial text at all.
- **~3–4 s:** Tapped a tray item (picked it up), then dragged it onto the highlighted lane — a block appeared above the gate and the resource counter dropped by the item's cost. Drag-to-place figured out purely by trial.
- **~16 s:** Dragging a second piece onto the first triggered a full-screen **merge chain reveal** popup showing 5 upgrade tiers of the cannon (plain → green → blue → purple → orange). This was the first real explanation of the core merge mechanic, and it happened by chance, not by design cue.
- **~17–21 s:** Camera zoomed into combat automatically, showing two goblins with damage-percent labels and a "TAP TO CONTINUE" prompt — an unexplained cutscene-like beat.
- **~22 s:** Curiosity-tapped a green diamond icon — opened a **Blueprints** shop (a permanent skill tree paid for with "star coins") with no in-context explanation of what it does or how currency is earned.
- **~24–41 s:** Discovered VOLLEY by tapping it blind (it fired a bombardment, self-evident from the explosion) and bought a "REINFORCED HAND" offer for 30 star coins, whose effect (a gold border on all 3 tray slots) was never explained.
- **~1–2 min:** Waves 2–5 cleared smoothly; flying reward chests appeared periodically with "FREE" items to place; resources climbed passively between waves.
- **~2.3 min (wave 7 clear):** The "CALL WAVE" button turned into a red **"CALL BOSS"** countdown — a clear, well-communicated signal.
- **~3.8 min (wave 10):** Discovered a **Daily Missions** panel (clipboard icon, red badge) with 3 pre-completed missions worth 200 star coins total — a big, satisfying reward burst, found only by curiosity-tapping a new icon.
- **~5.5–5.7 min (wave 13):** Wall visibly caught fire (a green flame icon) and later showed frost hits; the wall collapsed from full height to almost nothing in about 15 seconds under simultaneous attacks from goblins, skeletons, and flying units.
- **~5.7 min — RUN 1 END:** "Destroyed!" screen. "You survived to Wave 13 — NEW RECORD — Rank #217 of 361 — +571 star coins." No indication of what specifically caused the loss.

## Run 2 — what happened (same; what I did differently and why; how it ended)
- Before restarting, spent the 571+ star coins from Run 1 in Blueprints: Foundations, Sharpened Bolts, Rapid Fire, Gate Armour, and unlocked Spiked Wall and Bombard. This shop, for the first time, showed **tutorial tooltips** ("Everything you buy here is permanent — it carries into every future run", "Coins come from every siege, win or lose", and a gold/blue/dim color-code legend) — but only *after* my first death, quite late.
- The result screen also offered a one-time "**WAVE 12 +240 wood, 30 gems**" head-start button; I skipped it to test a normal restart.
- Started Run 2 knowing the drag-and-merge loop, so placed blocks faster and used VOLLEY and free chests proactively.
- **~356–365 s (game time):** The browser/controller connection dropped twice and had to be reconnected by the coordinator (**tester-side interruption, not a game finding**); the page reloaded to the same saved game each time with only a single un-placed block lost.
- Continued placing blocks, repairing, and buying Blueprints (Sharpened Bolts, Rapid Fire ranks) between waves.
- **~8 min (wave 8):** A new enemy type — a large tree/ent monster — appeared on the right flank and was destroyed by tower fire; never explained.
- **~9–10 min (waves 9–12):** Wave sizes grew fast (27, 28, then 34 enemies in wave 12); the tower (now taller thanks to permanent upgrades) still lost tiers under sustained multi-directional pressure, and the gate briefly flashed a red critical-damage outline.
- **~10.3 min — RUN 2 END (time-cap, not defeat):** Reached Wave 12 with 5 enemies left and the gate still standing but heavily worn; I stopped the run at the ~10-minute game-time cap as instructed, rather than push further. Compared to Run 1, Run 2's peak wave was slightly lower (12 vs 13) but the economy was much larger (502/290/109 in the three currencies) and the tower visibly taller — a real signal of session-to-session power growth from the permanent upgrades.

## Information gaps
1. **What to do on first load.** Saw: Wave 1 screen, tray, VOLLEY, no text. Didn't understand: that tapping then dragging places a piece. Stayed unclear: ~3–4 s (resolved by trial). Screenshot: `0002-wait.jpg`. Severity: **high** (a non-curious player could easily sit confused before touching anything).
2. **Merge mechanic.** Saw: a full merge-chain reveal popup. Didn't understand until it happened by chance that dropping one piece on another merges/upgrades it. Stayed unclear: ~16 s, and even after the popup, exactly which drop targets trigger a merge vs a normal placement was never stated. Screenshot: `0009-drag.jpg`. Severity: **medium**.
3. **VOLLEY ability.** Saw: a yellow button with a cooldown badge. Didn't understand what it does until tapped blind. Effect (bombardment) was self-evident once tried, but the resource/charge cost was never labeled. Screenshot: `0024-tap.jpg`. Severity: **low**.
4. **Blueprints / star-coin meta-shop.** Saw: a green diamond icon, tapped out of curiosity. Didn't understand this was the permanent-progression currency spend until tutorial tooltips appeared — but those tooltips only showed up **after my first death**, roughly 5.5 minutes and one full run into the session. Screenshot: `0014-tap.jpg` (discovery) vs `0150-tap.jpg` (tutorial, post-death). Severity: **high**.
5. **Four separate unlabeled resource currencies** (a wood/hammer icon, a stone/gem-piece icon, a star coin, and later a second currency for Bombard costing two icons at once) with no text names anywhere on screen. Never fully disambiguated which currency does what. Stayed unclear the entire session. Screenshot: `0010-shot.jpg`. Severity: **medium-high**.
6. **"REINFORCED HAND" purchase.** Saw: bought for 30 star coins; all 3 tray slots gained a gold border. Didn't understand the functional change (better pieces? free re-roll? no visible cost/benefit stated). Stayed unclear entire session. Screenshot: `0027-tap.jpg`. Severity: **medium**.
7. **Daily Missions panel.** Saw: clipboard icon with a red badge appeared after wave 10, containing 3 already-completed missions worth 200 star coins. Never introduced or flagged before that point — a player who doesn't explore the UI could miss a large reward entirely. Screenshot: `0102-tap.jpg`. Severity: **medium** (upside is high but only if found).
8. **Status effects on wall blocks** (green fire icon, blue "frozen" outline). Never explained what they do (damage-over-time? disabled weapon?) or how to counter them. Stayed unclear entire session. Screenshot: `0137-tap.jpg` (fire), `0027-tap` area (frost outline in run 2). Severity: **medium**.
9. **Cause of death / wall collapse.** Saw: the wall going from full height to rubble in ~15 s under combined fire/frost/flying-unit pressure with a "Destroyed!" screen and no post-mortem breakdown (which block failed first, which enemy dealt the most damage). Stayed unclear through both runs. Screenshot: `0142-wait.jpg` → `0147-wait.jpg`. Severity: **high** — without this, a player can't clearly plan around it next run.
10. **Enemy path / where they come from.** Enemies simply appear from off-screen left and right with no visible road/path indicator beyond a vague ground texture. Never explained. Severity: **low**.

## Incentive gaps
1. **Post-death "WAVE 12 head start" offer.** Cost 30 gems, one-time-per-day, with no stated benefit beyond "start further in." I couldn't tell if this was a good deal relative to a fresh run's Blueprints spending, so I skipped it — the choice felt arbitrary rather than informed. Severity: medium.
2. **No visible top-level goal beyond "survive."** There's no stated win condition, score target, or "here's what wave 20 gets you" framing during play — only after death does a "NEW RECORD / Rank #217 of 361" appear, which is the single strongest motivational hook in the whole experience, but it shows up too late (only at the moment you've already lost) rather than being visible as a target during the run.
3. **Meta-shop visits aren't prompted during a run.** The Blueprints shop only got a small red badge notification once (after a Daily Missions unlock); most of the time nothing on-screen reminds the player they have spendable star coins sitting idle, so it's easy to just keep playing without ever spending.
4. **Rank/leaderboard teaser with no follow-through.** "Rank #217 of 361" implies a leaderboard exists, but nothing let me view or explore it — a dangling incentive that could have driven more replay motivation if tappable.

## What worked (things that were clear or motivating)
- **Flying "FREE" reward chests** — simple, unambiguous, and satisfying; understood on first sight.
- **The merge chain reveal popup** — a genuinely exciting "oh, THAT'S how this works" moment, even though it was one-time and accidental.
- **VOLLEY's payoff** — the explosion and kill-reward numbers made the button's value obvious without any text.
- **WAVE → CALL BOSS transition** — this was the clearest piece of UI communication in the whole game: a plain color and label change that accurately signaled rising stakes.
- **Daily Missions currency dump** — a strong dopamine spike (200 star coins in one tap) that immediately made me want to go spend it.
- **Permanent Blueprints progression** — once understood, it gave a concrete, visible reason to want to keep playing across runs (a taller, tougher tower each time).
- **"NEW RECORD" + rank number at death** — effective, if late-arriving, competitive hook that made we want to beat wave 13 again.

## Quit points (moments a real player like me would likely have closed the tab; game time + reason)
- **~0–5 s, first load:** A less curious player than my persona could plausibly bounce here — there is genuinely zero on-screen instruction for what to do with the tray or the highlighted lane.
- **~5.7 min, Run 1 death:** The sudden, unexplained wall collapse (fire + frost + swarm simultaneously, no combat log) is the kind of "I don't know what I did wrong" moment that drives real players away rather than making them want to retry, even though the death screen's "NEW RECORD" framing partially offset this for me.
- I did not personally quit in either run — the persona's curiosity about the Blueprints shop and Daily Missions kept me going — but I'd flag both moments above as real risk points for a less exploratory player.

## Would you play a 3rd run? (1–5, why, and the single change that would raise it)
**4/5.** The build-and-merge tower defense loop is fun and the permanent-progression shop gives real pull, but I'm still fuzzy on exactly what killed my tower both times, which caps my confidence that a 3rd run would go noticeably better. **Single highest-impact change:** a short first-run tutorial overlay (even 3–4 tap-to-dismiss cards) that (a) shows the drag-to-place-and-merge gesture once, and (b) points at the Blueprints shop and explains "star coins persist between runs, spend them here" — both currently learned only by accident or after a death.
