# Playtester 1 — desktop, persona: casual portal browser

## Summary (5 lines max)
The merge/tower-defense loop is taught well in the first 10 seconds (a clear visual diagram of cannon merging) and the first placement/upgrade is guided step by step. After that, waves 1-13 played themselves with almost no danger or decisions, which is boring for an impatient portal player. Wave 14 introduced a "cavalry out of range" enemy that dropped my health from full to zero in about 10-15 seconds with only a one-line warning — my only real death threat all session, and it caused Run 1's defeat. The death screen (New Record, global rank, currency reward, a free "continue") was genuinely motivating and pulled me straight into a second attempt, which ended in a second defeat at wave 17 after another out-of-reach cavalry rush overwhelmed my (now-upgraded) tower. (A browser crash on the test rig interrupted the session for several minutes partway through Run 2; the browser was reopened on the same save and play resumed normally — this was a tooling issue, not a game finding.)

## Run 1 — what happened
- **~0-2s (first impression):** Game was already on a loading screen at 100%; next frame dropped me straight into Wave 0 with a "Monsters Incoming!" banner, resources shown (120 wood / 40 stone / 0 gold-like currency) and a yellow arrow pointing at a highlighted card labeled "REINFORCED HAND 30". No title/menu/goal screen was shown to me at any point.
- **~3-11s:** Clicking the arrowed card opened a placement grid on the tower; clicking the highlighted cell triggered an unprompted popup diagram showing 2 basic cannons merge into tier 2 (green), 2 tier-2 into tier 3 (blue), etc. Immediately after, a side-by-side comparison showed a basic cannon doing -10% damage vs. a tier-3 cannon doing -75% damage to the same enemy. This taught me "it's a merge game" and "merging = much stronger" very efficiently.
- **~11-13s:** Guided click on the cannon opened its info panel (HP/Armour/Damage/Cooldown/Range/Splash) with an arrow pointing at an UPGRADE button; upgrading worked immediately and clearly (LV1→LV2, damage/HP visibly increased).
- **~13-20s:** Arrow guided me to CALL WAVE. Wave 1 (3 enemies) resolved almost entirely on its own; "WAVE 1 HELD! +8%" appeared with growing resources.
- **~20-50s:** Explored hand-card placement (wood crates, archery combo pieces, stone blocks, a lumber hut). Discovered valid (green) vs invalid (red) placement highlighting by trial and error. A "FREE" card with a "REINFORCED HAND 30" label appeared floating above the hand row and never did anything when clicked or dragged — still unexplained by end of session.
- **~75s:** By chance, opened Daily Missions via a clipboard icon with a small badge — three missions were already complete (earn coins, survive wave 1, place 15 blocks) and claiming all three gave 200 currency in a satisfying coin-flying animation.
- **~150-230s (waves 5-9):** Repetitive: watch cannon auto-fire, occasionally place a hand card or spend currency in the Tech Tree (found via a diamond icon, also arrow-guided the first time). Health bar never dropped from full the entire time. Bought several Tech Tree nodes (Foundations +HP%, Sharpened Bolts +damage%) since currency was abundant.
- **~222s:** CALL WAVE button turned into a red CALL BOSS button with no explanation of what a boss is. Wave 10 boss died without me noticing any special behavior or taking real damage.
- **~269-330s (waves 11-13):** Individual tower blocks (crates, roofs) visibly disappeared/were destroyed between waves, but the health bar at top-left stayed full or fully refilled between waves regardless — I never got an explanation of how block loss relates to the health bar.
- **~354s (critical moment):** A banner "OUT OF REACH - RIDE OUT OR EXTEND YOUR RANGE" appeared alongside two buttons, "+80% RANGE 30" and "CAVALRY 40". My health bar had already collapsed from full to near-zero in the few seconds before I understood what was happening — a mounted enemy was standing just past my cannon's range, hitting the tower with nothing able to hit back.
- **~357s — RUN 1 END:** Tower/cannon greyed out, defeat cutscene (goblins celebrating in front of a reduced tower), then result screen: "DESTROYED! YOU SURVIVED TO WAVE 14", a "NEW RECORD" badge, +300 currency reward, "Best Score 232" (lower than wave 14, metric unclear), "Rank #234 of 361", a gameplay tip ("Lumber huts and stone pits pay you every wave..."), and a green "WAVE 13 +100/+100/30 [1x]" button plus two icon buttons (tech tree, restart).

## Run 2 — what happened
Clicking the green "WAVE 13 +100/+100/30 [1x]" button on the death screen did not restart the game — it fully revived the tower at wave 13 with restored health and bonus resources (a free one-time "continue," not the fast-forward/skip its label suggested). Since this was what the game offered immediately after defeat, I used it as my second attempt (per instructions to "use whatever the game offers after a defeat"), rather than reloading to a blank state:
- **Wave 14 (2nd time):** The same "OUT OF REACH" cavalry warning appeared again, but this time CAVALRY was offered for FREE (not 40 currency). Deploying it let my mounted unit ride out and fight back — I survived with a sliver of health. This was the first time a mechanic I'd struggled with became clear and satisfying to use.
- **Wave 15:** 44 enemies, a very close call (health down to a thin red sliver) but survived. Noticed the 3x speed toggle cost 30 currency this time when it had been freely toggleable earlier — unclear why.
- **Wave 16:** Tower lost its entire left wing (lumber hut, houses) to enemy damage; health again went critical then fully refilled by wave 17, reinforcing that block loss and the health bar are two separate systems. Spent 30 currency on a persistent "+80% RANGE" button proactively, hoping to blunt the next out-of-reach rush.
- **[Interruption]:** The playtest controller then stopped responding ("controller not reachable / fetch failed") for several minutes. This turned out to be a browser crash on the test rig, not a game issue — the browser was relaunched on the same saved game and resumed exactly where it left off (wave 16, same resources). Noted here for transparency; excluded from gameplay findings.
- **Wave 17 (35→0 enemies) — RUN 2 END:** Despite the range upgrade, the tower was overwhelmed and destroyed. Result screen: "DESTROYED! YOU SURVIVED TO WAVE 17" (up from wave 14 in Run 1), another "NEW RECORD" badge, +373 currency, "Best Score 350" (again a different, lower number than the wave reached), "Rank #164 of 361" (a clear, visible rank improvement from Run 1's #234 — strong progress signal), a new tip ("Banners boost every block they touch — and they multiply. Surround one gun instead of spreading them out."), and the same "WAVE 15 +100/+100/30 [1x]" continue offer. I stopped here, having reached the second defeat.

## Information gaps
1. **REINFORCED HAND / "FREE" floating card** — Seen continuously from ~20s to session end (444s+), a badge counter climbing (1→3+) with no explanation. Clicking and dragging it did nothing observable the entire session. Severity: medium (persistent, unresolved mystery UI element).
2. **Flying winged treasure chest** — First seen ~20s, recurs throughout; unclear if/how to collect it or what it contains. It once appeared to "land" on the tower with no visible effect. Severity: low-medium.
3. **Green "+N currency" claim buttons (bottom-left)** — Appeared periodically; one click at ~127s seemed to *decrease* my currency by 2 rather than add it, contradicting the "+" label. Behavior felt inconsistent. Severity: medium.
4. **CALL BOSS button** — Appeared at ~222s replacing CALL WAVE with zero explanation of what makes a boss different. Severity: low-medium.
5. **Tower block destruction vs. health bar relationship** — Individual crates/roofs visibly disappeared across waves 11, 16 while the top-left health bar independently emptied and fully refilled between waves. Never explained; had to infer it from repeated observation. This affects understanding of the core lose-condition. Severity: medium-high.
6. **"OUT OF REACH" cavalry threat** — The single most impactful gap. First appeared ~354s with my health already nearly gone; I had no prior warning that some enemies (mounted units) stay outside cannon range and can't be hit without a separate response (Cavalry or +Range). This directly caused my Run 1 defeat. Severity: high.
7. **3x game-speed toggle cost** — Free the first time I used it (~90s), then cost 30 currency later (~406s) with no explanation of the change. Severity: low-medium.
8. **"Roofed — needs clear sky" placement rule** — Several hand cards (wood crate combos) showed red/invalid placement in many spots I tried, and I could not tell why until reading the tooltip text closely; wasted several actions hunting for a valid cell. Severity: medium.
9. **Death-screen "WAVE 13/15 +100/+100/30 [1x]" button** — Its label reads like a fast-forward/skip reward; clicking it actually fully revived my tower and restored health, which I only discovered after clicking. Severity: medium.
10. **"Best Score" vs. "Survived to Wave N"** — Both death screens showed a "Best Score" number lower than the wave reached (232 vs wave 14; 350 vs wave 17), with no stated relationship between the two metrics. Severity: low.
11. **Banner tip on Run 2's death screen** — "Banners boost every block they touch — and they multiply. Surround one gun instead of spreading them out." referenced a "banner" item/mechanic I never knowingly placed or saw explained during either run. Severity: low.

## Incentive gaps
1. **Passive mid-game (waves ~1-13, roughly 30s-330s)** — Health bar never dropped, there was very little for me to actively decide or react to beyond occasionally spending resources; mostly watched the cannon auto-fire. For a low-patience portal player this is a real risk of tab-closing before anything dangerous or exciting happens. Severity: high.
2. **Resource surplus with no big sink** — By wave 12-15 I was holding 400-700 wood/stone and 200-900 currency with only small hand-card costs (20-90) and modest tech-tree node costs (20-140) to spend on; no "big ticket" purchase ever appeared to create a meaningful spending decision. Severity: medium.
3. **Daily Missions found by accident** — A genuinely strong, clear incentive (three missions, clear rewards, satisfying claim animation) but I only found it because I clicked an unlabeled clipboard icon with a small badge; there was no tutorial nudge toward it the way there was for the hand card, cannon upgrade, and tech tree. A less exploratory player could easily miss it entirely. Severity: low-medium.
4. **Tech Tree gated just out of reach early** — On first opening it at ~37s I had 10 currency against a 20-currency first node; nothing signaled "come back when you have enough," so it felt like a dead end rather than a goal to work toward.
5. **Post-defeat pull was strong, and repeatable** — Both death screens showed a "NEW RECORD" badge, a global rank (which visibly improved from #234 to #164 of 361), a currency reward, a specific gameplay tip, and a free one-time full continue. Seeing the rank climb between the two defeats was a concrete, legible "you're getting better" signal — the single best-designed motivational moment in the session (see "What worked").
6. **Buying the "+80% Range" upgrade didn't visibly prevent the next cavalry rush** — I spent 30 currency proactively on the range boost after learning about the out-of-reach threat, but the tower was still destroyed by wave 17's rush; there was no feedback confirming the upgrade helped (or by how much), which undercuts the incentive to buy defensive upgrades ahead of time rather than reactively.

## What worked
- The merge-chain diagram and damage comparison shown in the first ~10 seconds taught the core mechanic (merge cannons = much more damage) faster and more clearly than text ever would.
- Arrow-guided first actions (select card → place → upgrade → call wave) were unambiguous and easy to follow with a mouse.
- Green/red placement highlighting made "can I put this here" visually obvious once I noticed it (even though *why* something was invalid wasn't always clear).
- Daily Missions reward screen: clear goals, clear rewards, satisfying to claim.
- Death screen combo (new record + global rank + currency reward + free continue) made me want to keep playing immediately rather than close the tab.
- Once explained by trial, the Cavalry counter to the out-of-range threat was a genuinely fun "aha" moment and the most engaging single decision of the whole session.

## Quit points
- **~2-4 minutes into Run 1 (waves 5-9):** This is where my "low patience portal browser" persona would most likely have closed the tab. The tutorial hook had worn off, nothing threatened the tower, and there was little to actively do besides occasionally clicking a hand card. I only kept going because I was actively investigating UI elements (tech tree, missions) out of curiosity — a typical skimming player might not have bothered.
- **~354s (Run 1's out-of-reach death):** Losing suddenly to a mechanic with almost no warning could also read as "unfair" to a first-time player and cause a quit-in-frustration, if the death screen's rewards hadn't immediately offset that feeling.
- **After Run 2's defeat (wave 17):** Having now died twice to the same unresolved-feeling mechanic (out-of-reach cavalry) despite spending currency on a defensive upgrade in between, a real player could reasonably conclude "there's no way to actually fix this" and quit here rather than try a third time — the rank improvement and reward soften this, but the repeat death to the same cause is a real risk.

## Would you play a 3rd run? (1-5, why, and the single change that would raise it)
**3/5.** The reward loop (rank climbing from #234 to #164, a new record both times, currency) is a real pull, and I'd probably tap "continue" once more out of curiosity. But dying twice to the same "out of reach" mechanic — even after spending currency specifically to counter it — makes it feel like luck or wave composition rather than a skill I'm improving at, which caps my enthusiasm. The single change that would raise this most: make defensive purchases (like the Range upgrade) visibly and reliably solve the problem they're sold against, and introduce the out-of-reach threat much earlier (wave 3-4 instead of wave 14) so it's a known, practiced mechanic well before the 5-6 minute mark rather than a late surprise that repeats unresolved.
