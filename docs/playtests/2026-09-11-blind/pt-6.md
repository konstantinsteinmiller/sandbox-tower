# Playtester 6 — mobile, persona: hyper-casual phone player

## Summary (5 lines max)
Tower Siege is a wave-based castle-defense builder: drag "cards" from a hand onto small dashed slots on a wall to build cannons/archers/walls that auto-fight incoming monsters, plus a manual VOLLEY button and a permanent "Blueprints" upgrade tree bought with a separate star currency. The core loop (drag card onto slot) took me about 48 real seconds of blind trial-and-error to discover because my first instinct — dropping units into the big highlighted monster lane — does nothing there. Both runs ended at the same wave (14) via slow attrition of my buildings that was never clearly explained (small green health bars I mostly didn't notice, and permanent loss of a slot once its building's HP hits 0). The best moment was finding the Blueprints permanent-upgrade menu and a very generous "continue from last wave" offer after my first death; the worst was dying at the identical wave in run 2 despite spending on upgrades, which undercut the feeling that progression mattered.

## Run 1 — what happened
- **~0s (load):** Logo/loading screen, then straight into "WAVE 1·1 — MONSTERS INCOMING!" with a highlighted lane, no goal text, no tutorial, top bar shows three unexplained numbers/icons (wood, puzzle-piece, gold-coin).
- **~5–45s:** Tried tapping the bottom "cards" (10/20/39 cost) directly — nothing happened. Tried dragging one into the highlighted monster lane — nothing visible placed, though resources moved and the wave auto-completed ("WAVE 1 HELD!"). Genuinely confused about what I had just done.
- **~48s:** Discovered by chance that dragging a card onto one of the small dashed squares next to the castle (not the lane) places a building; the hand then refreshes with a new, pricier card. This is the whole mechanic, but the game never says so.
- **~77s:** Discovered VOLLEY (bottom-left button) by tapping it once it visually looked "active" — it's a manual damage burst with a ~3s cooldown. No tooltip anywhere told me this.
- **~108s (wave 6 held):** Poked the "..." menu during a lull and found "Blueprints," a permanent star-currency upgrade tree (Sharpened Bolts, Braced Crates, Foundations, Bombard, etc.). Bought my first upgrade. This was the first moment I felt real motivation/progress.
- **~147s (wave 8, boss):** First boss enemy (a big tree creature). Used VOLLEY, won, got a big reward jump (+~30 stars, +~100 wood/puzzle). Noticed afterward that a whole column of my buildings had vanished — the boss (or accumulated hits) had destroyed them permanently.
- **~215–306s (waves 9–13):** Repeating cycle: rebuild destroyed slots with wood, buy a Blueprint upgrade when I had spare stars, watch buildings get whittled down again almost every wave. By wave 13 I was often down to 1–2 columns out of what had been 3.
- **~355–362s (wave 14):** Last enemy (a wolf) got stuck fighting my one remaining cannon for a long time; I placed one more archer to help. Shortly after, "Destroyed!" — **Run 1 ended at wave 14**, rank #308 of 361, +264 stars.

## Run 2 — what happened
Different from a instructed from-scratch restart: the death screen offered a green "WAVE 13 +240 wood / 30 star" button that resumed the game at wave 13 with **all buildings fully rebuilt/healed for free** and a wood bonus — I used it since it's literally what the game offered after defeat. Before continuing I explored the death-screen shop-cart icon, which reopened Blueprints and, for the first time, showed onboarding tooltips explaining the permanent-upgrade system and the coin/star economy (see Information Gap 1). I spent the accumulated stars (334) on Bombard, Foundations rank 3, and Rapid Fire.
- **~370–393s (wave 14 continue):** Resumed with a full 3-column castle. New Bombard building type appeared in the hand (costs both wood AND puzzle pieces). Fought through most of wave 14, buildings degrading again despite the upgrades.
- **~402s:** "Destroyed!" again — **Run 2 ended at wave 14**, rank #306 of 361 (essentially identical outcome to run 1), +238 stars. This time no continue button appeared, only shop-cart and a skip-arrow icon.
- **Post-run 2:** Tapped skip-arrow, which starts a genuine fresh run from wave 1 (wood/puzzle reset, star currency persists at 250). Confirmed the star currency is the only thing that truly carries forward.

## Information gaps
1. **What the bottom cards are and where they go.** Saw the three "cards" with cost numbers from the very first frame (0s); did not understand they are draggable buildings that must be dropped on small dashed slots (not the monster lane) until ~48s of failed attempts. Screenshot: `0004-tap.jpg`, `0005-drag.jpg`. Severity: **high** — this is the entire core interaction and nothing on screen hints at it.
2. **VOLLEY ability purpose/cooldown.** Visible from 0s but greyed out; no label ever explains it deals burst damage or that it's on a cooldown timer. Discovered by trial at ~77s. Screenshot: `0032-tap.jpg`. Severity: **medium**.
3. **Top bar currencies (wood/puzzle-piece/star icons).** Never labeled anywhere in-session. I inferred wood = building cost, puzzle piece = repair/reinforce cost, star = permanent-upgrade currency, purely from watching numbers change — never confirmed by explicit text until the post-death tooltip at ~363s ("Coins come from every siege..."). Screenshot: `0002-wait.jpg` (first appearance) vs `0106-tap.jpg` (first explanation). Severity: **medium**.
4. **Buildings can be permanently destroyed.** Green health bars under buildings are small and easy to miss; when a building's HP reaches 0 it disappears from the board entirely (the slot has to be rebuilt with wood from scratch). This first became obvious at wave 8 (boss) and repeated almost every wave after. No message, warning, or clear health-bar callout ever explained this is happening or why. Screenshots: `0052-wait.jpg` (before) vs `0057-wait.jpg` (after — whole column gone). Severity: **high** — this is the actual lose condition driving both defeats, and it was never legible.
5. **Blueprints/permanent-upgrade menu is buried.** Found only by chance at ~108s (2 taps deep: "..." then a small document icon with a badge). No prompt ever told me it exists or that it's worth checking. Screenshot: `0043-tap.jpg`. Severity: **high** given it's the main incentive structure of the game.
6. **Tutorial tooltips for Blueprints appear too late.** The explanatory tooltips ("Everything you buy here is permanent," "Coins come from every siege," node color meanings) only triggered the first time I opened the shop from the post-death screen — after I had already used the menu three times, unexplained, during run 1. Screenshots: `0105-tap.jpg`, `0106-tap.jpg`, `0107-tap.jpg`. Severity: **medium**.
7. **The vertical slider top-right of the HUD.** Present from the very first screenshot, never responded to tap or drag, and I never worked out its function (possibly day/wave progress). Severity: **low** (didn't block play, but is a piece of UI real estate that does nothing observable).
8. **Merge mechanic exists but is unlabeled.** Placing a 3rd matching building triggered an "x3" merge effect upgrading it into a new type (e.g., the Braced Crate). This happened once, unexplained, at ~230s. Screenshot: `0075-drag.jpg`. Severity: **medium** — I couldn't reliably plan around merging because I never saw a rule stated.
9. **Why the death-screen "continue from wave 13" offer appeared once and never again.** No timer, cost, or ad prompt was shown to explain its availability or absence in run 2's death screen. Severity: **medium**.

## Incentive gaps
1. **No stated goal at any point.** The game never says what "winning" looks like (survive forever? reach a specific wave? beat all bosses?) — I inferred "survive as long as possible" purely from the wave counter and "Destroyed!" screen. Severity: **medium** — hyper-casual players want an explicit target almost immediately.
2. **Progression didn't visibly pay off between runs.** Despite buying 5 permanent Blueprint upgrades (Sharpened Bolts x2, Braced Crates, Foundations x3, Bombard, Rapid Fire) between the wave-13 continue and finishing run 2, I died at essentially the same wave/rank as run 1 (wave 14, rank 308 → rank 306). As a first-time player this made the star-shop feel like it wasn't meaningfully changing my odds, which is deflating right after the first "aha" of discovering it.
3. **Building destruction outpaced my ability to rebuild for most of the mid-game.** From wave 8 onward I was in a near-constant catch-up cycle (lose a column, rebuild it, lose it again) rather than feeling like I was getting ahead. There was no moment after wave 8 where my defenses felt stronger than the wave in front of them — the tension never resolved into a feeling of mastery.
4. **The reward for a boss kill (wave 8) was the single biggest motivation spike in the whole session** (+30 stars, +100ish wood/puzzle in one hit) but that peak was never repeated at a similar scale, so momentum dropped off afterward.
5. **The rank number ("#308 of 361") is a nice light-competitive hook** but it's shown for half a second on a screen with several other numbers competing for attention, and it isn't explained (of what? all-time players? today's players?).

## What worked
- Placing a building card and seeing the coin/puzzle-piece deduction plus a physical object appear on the wall (once I discovered it) was satisfying and readable.
- The star-currency permanent upgrade tree (Blueprints) is a genuinely well-designed hook once you find it — clear cost/effect text, gold/blue/locked visual states, and the explicit "this carries into every future run" promise are exactly the kind of clarity that motivates a session-to-session player.
- VOLLEY's burst damage number pop (e.g., "+21", "+33") gave a satisfying, readable hit of impact.
- The boss wave (wave 8) was a clear escalation beat with a distinct visual (tree creature, health bar) and a big payout — the best-paced moment of either run.
- The post-death "continue from last wave, fully rebuilt" offer (run 1 to run 2) is a very player-friendly, low-friction way to reduce death sting — when it's available.

## Quit points
- **~45–50s into run 1:** After ~5 failed taps/drags with zero visible feedback, a less patient real player (my persona included) would likely have closed the tab here, concluding the game "doesn't work" rather than that they hadn't found the drop zone yet. I only kept going because the assignment required it.
- **~402s (end of run 2):** Dying at the exact same wave as run 1, after having spent real effort finding and buying permanent upgrades, is the point where a real player's "I'm getting better" belief gets punctured. Combined with no continue offer this time, this is a natural quit point for a casual player who came in expecting quick, escalating wins.

## Would you play a 3rd run? (1–5, why, and the single change that would raise it)
**2/5.** The moment-to-moment building/shooting is pleasant enough for a few minutes, and the Blueprints shop is a real hook, but I hit an unexplained core mechanic wall in the first minute and an unexplained lose condition (buildings silently vanish) for the rest of both runs, and my two runs ended identically despite spending currency in between — nothing told me I was actually getting stronger. The single change that would raise this most: put a one-time, un-skippable arrow/highlight on the very first empty wall slot the first time a card is picked up (so the drag target is obvious immediately), and give every building a clearly visible red/cracking state before it's destroyed rather than a small green sliver that's easy to miss.
