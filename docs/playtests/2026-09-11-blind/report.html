Tower Siege Blind Playtest

Ten first-time players who had never seen the game, five on a desktop browser with a mouse and five on a phone with touch, each played two runs. Each tester saw only screenshots and had only a mouse or a finger. This report covers where they lacked information and where they lacked a reason to keep playing.

10 testers · 20 runs · 91 min of game time Claude Sonnet testers, persona-driven Desktop 1280×720 · Phone 390×844 touch (Drop & Merge)

People like the fight once it gets going. Eight of ten testers praised the Call Wave → Call Boss escalation, and all ten called the permanent upgrade tree the best reason to keep playing. Engagement is lost at the edges, before the loop and between runs.

On the phone, the first tutorial beat has no words and blocks the card tray, so no phone tester ever completed it. That means none of them was taught merging. On desktop, a rejected placement makes no visible sign. On both, the upgrade system is explained only after the first death, and the defeat screen relies on unlabeled icons and a rank that never moves.

No tester beat their first run on the second attempt, despite buying 1–15 upgrade ranks. Several took that as proof the upgrades don't matter.

What drives players away

Six findings, worst first

Who hit it: desktop tester phone tester didn't
CriticalPhone 5 / 5 phone never finished it

The phone's first lesson blocks what players touch first

The opening coach mark dims the whole screen except one column, has no words, and swallows every touch outside that column, including the card tray. The piece is already selected for the player, so the only action that works is tapping the column. Every phone tester touched the cards first.

  • All five read the lit column as "the lane the monsters come down", not "drop here".
  • P6 took about 48 s to place anything. P10 decided the game had frozen and reloaded it.
  • The phone board doesn't hold the build clock for its coach marks, so wave 1 plays out underneath. The fort clears it alone ("WAVE 1 HELD!"), and testers couldn't tell whether they had done anything.
  • The lesson ends when wave 3 starts but is never marked done, so it comes back on every fresh run and every reload, dimming the tray again. P10 concluded that dragging to build "broke after a reload".
  • Because the drop beat was never completed, the merge ladder and the power demo never played on any phone. Merging was found by accident (P7's "x3", P8 misplacing a piece) or not at all.
  • The auto-armed piece has a side effect: any tap on the board buys and drops it. P9 and P10 built a "tap enemies to attack" mental model, since a dropped block reads "CRUSHED!", and drained their materials.
Fix

Add one line of text to the beat ("Tap the glowing column to drop your piece"), or let the tray be the target and the column the destination. Hold the wave clock while the beat is up. When the stage ends, retire the lesson instead of re-arming it. If the drop beat never completed, still show the merge ladder on the first merge. TutorialOverlay.vue rules 1–3, GameSceneDrop.vue:878–892, :942–956.

CriticalBoth 10 / 10

The best reason to come back is found last

Every tester called the permanent upgrade tree (Tech Tree on desktop, Blueprints on phone) the strongest hook. Nobody was told it existed or that it persists until after they had died.

  • Phone: the tree lives in the … drawer and behind the unlabeled cart icon on the defeat screen. P7, P8 and P10 found it only after their second defeat, and P9 after its first. Three of five bought a single rank, at the very end. Phone testers finished holding 250–658 unspent coins.
  • Desktop: the "SPEND!" nudge works, and all five bought upgrades in run 1. But the rules (what's permanent, where coins come from, what gold/blue/dim nodes mean) only arrive with the three-step intro, and for every tester that ran after their first death.
  • That intro was the most praised piece of copy in the game. It just runs too late to shape the first run.
Fix

Run the three-step intro the first time the player can afford a node, not the first time the tree is opened. On phone, move the tree out of the drawer: a HUD chip with the affordable badge. At the first coin payout, say once "Coins are kept forever. Spend them on upgrades." GameSceneDrop.vue:1488–1512.

CriticalBoth 10 / 10

The defeat screen doesn't sell the next run

This is the moment to recruit run 2, and the screen asks players to decode it.

  • Icon-only choices. "Upgrade" and "Defend again" are a cart and a skip glyph. Phone testers expected the cart to open a store.
  • A rank that never moves. The rank is the rank of the player's best score, by design. Desktop shows this run's score beside it; the phone screen shows only "Rank #316 of 361". P7, P8 and P10 saw the same number after a worse run and decided it was fake. P8's first rank, #352, sat right under "NEW RECORD!".
  • A record on the wrong number. On desktop "NEW RECORD!" sits beside the coin payout, and D1 and D4 read +223 and +267 as scores.
  • Tips about things the player can't build. Lumber huts and stone pits are locked in the tech tree, and the tip doesn't say so. "Banners" are named, but the flag card never shows that name. D2 put it as "you lost because of a thing we never told you."
  • A revive nobody understood. It appeared once and then never again, sometimes free and sometimes 30 coins, with no text. Some read it as an ad, some as a bug, some as a new run.
Fix

Caption the two actions ("Upgrades", "New run"). Put "Best" and this run's score next to the rank on the phone. Move "NEW RECORD!" next to the wave or score, not the coins. Only tip about things the player can use now; for locked content, say where it unlocks. State the revive's terms on the button ("Revive at wave 9 · once per run"). GameScene.vue:2610–2716, GameSceneDrop.vue:1551–1612.

HighBoth 9 / 10

Losing is illegible

Testers couldn't read their own health or see pieces fall, and no screen says why a run ended.

  • Desktop: the Gate's health bar, top left under "WAVE", has no label and reads as a wave-progress meter. D3 misread it for the whole first run, about 370 s, and D5 never worked it out.
  • Phone: there's no Gate health on the HUD at all. That's deliberate (TopStrip.vue), but P10 "had no persistent HP readout", and every phone death "felt sudden".
  • Pieces vanish between glances with no marker ("my entire tower vanished except the door", P8). Seven testers noticed losses only by comparing screenshots.
  • Nothing covers the case where nothing can hit the attackers. P7 at wave 11 and P9 at wave 15 had no weapon that could reach, saw no weapon they could afford, and watched enemies stand still for 20–45 s, reading it as a freeze. Desktop has an "out of reach" hint; the phone scene doesn't.
Fix

Label the desktop bar as the Gate, with an icon and a number. On phone, flash a vignette or show a small Gate chip when the Gate is hit. Mark each lost piece where it fell ("Archer lost"). Bring the desktop's "nothing can reach this" line to the phone, pointing at a weapon card or Volley. Name the killer on the defeat screen ("The Gate fell to the Treant").

HighDesktop 5 / 5 desktop

A rejected placement looks like nothing happened

An illegal placement plays a quiet sound (playSound('obstacle-hit', 0.03)) and shows nothing else. The red ghost of the armed piece stays where the pointer was, so testers read it as a placed block that later "vanished", or was "sold".

  • The blue outline cells show the general build area, not the cells this piece can actually use.
  • It cost two runs: D1 couldn't place a second piece and fell at wave 5, and D2 believed it had accidentally sold a block before wave 8.
  • The rule, that pieces need support from something already built, was never shown. Testers inferred it after 10–15 s of failed clicks.
Fix

Show the reason at the pointer: a shake plus "Needs support below" or "Not enough stone". Highlight only the anchors legal for the armed shape. GameScene.vue:438–456, :638–643.

MediumBoth 9 / 9 · run 2 no better (D3's run 2 was a deliberate test)

Upgrades don't show up as further runs

Desktop runs end at the wave-10 boss and phone runs cluster at the wave-8 boss. No tester's second run beat their first. The pre-boss warning is good ("Call Boss" in red); what's missing is what to do about the boss.

  • D5: "I entered run 2 with strictly more permanent upgrades … yet survived 3 fewer waves, and the game offered zero explanation."
  • P6 bought five upgrades between runs and died at the same wave. P10 said the boss "felt like a hard gate rather than a skill check".
  • Upgrades are percentages the player never sees in play. Only D1 and D2 got a visible payoff: 140 starting wood instead of 120.
Fix

When "Call Boss" appears, show one card: the boss, what it's weak to, and what the player has that answers it. After a boss death, say what would have helped. Put upgrade effects on the thing they change (a +14% badge on weapon stats), so run 2 visibly benefits.

Keep these

What already works

The upgrade tree, once found

Clear costs, visible states, and "carries into every future run". Everyone named it as the hook.

Call Wave turning into Call Boss

The best signal in the game: wordless, and it made players prepare.

The desktop opening

Place, merge ladder, −10% vs −75% on a real enemy, the stat panel, then Call Wave. Every desktop tester understood merging within about 15 s.

The missions coin shower

"The biggest single reward moment" when claimed. The payoff works; finding it doesn't.

Combat feedback

Damage numbers, "WAVE 7 HELD!", the green "x3" merge tag, "CRUSHED!". Readable and satisfying.

Carry-over you can see

Starting wood 120 → 140 after Lumber Stockpile was "the single clearest 'your time was not wasted' moment". Do more of this.

How this was run

Method and caveats

  • Testers. Ten Claude Sonnet agents, each with a persona and no knowledge of the game. They saw only screenshots and could only click, drag, scroll and hover (desktop) or tap, long-press, drag and pinch (phone), at screenshot coordinates.
  • Browsers. Ten headed Chrome windows, each on its own fresh profile, driven over the DevTools protocol. Phones were emulated at 390×844, touch, Android user agent; the fit resolver chose Drop & Merge for all five.
  • Build. A frozen, unobfuscated production build of the working tree on 11 Sep 2026, uncommitted tutorial changes included, served locally. It was the generic web build: no portal SDK and no ads (rewarded placements ran through the free-reward and coin paths).
  • Time. Game time was held while a tester thought. Each input let about 0.9 s pass, and waits ran up to 15 s. Partway through, the cap was cut from 15 to 10 game-minutes per run and longer waits were encouraged.
  • Leaderboard. Requests were blocked, so no test score reached the live board. Ranks came from the game's baked fallback.
  • No audio. Every window was muted, so sound-only cues such as the rejection click never reached the testers. Many portal players play muted too.
  • No cursor in screenshots. The desktop red placement ghost follows the pointer; without a visible pointer it looked like a stray block. That inflated finding 5's "vanished" and "sold" readings, though the missing visual reason is real.
  • Sampling. Testers looked every 1–12 s, so short toasts were easier to miss than for a human watching continuously. They also had unlimited thinking time, so real players under time pressure would likely do worse.
  • Persistence. The testers kept going because they were told to. Treat the quit points as where a real player would leave, not as observed churn.
  • Verified in code. Each finding above was checked against the screenshots and source before inclusion. Three tester claims were reclassified: the "phantom slot bug" and the "accidental sell" were both the rejected-placement ghost, and "tap enemies to attack" was the armed piece dropping.
  • Stability. No window logged a JavaScript exception in 91 game-minutes across both boards.
