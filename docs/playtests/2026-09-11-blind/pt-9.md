# Playtester 9 — mobile, persona: short-session commuter

## Summary (5 lines max)
A tower-defense game where you tap enemies to kill them and drag/tap cards to build a vertical stack of towers that auto-fire. Zero tutorial text appeared in either run — every mechanic (tap-to-kill, drag-to-build, REPAIR ALL, REINFORCED HAND, VOLLEY, the coin-star meta-currency and its permanent "Blueprints" upgrade shop) had to be reverse-engineered by trial and error, and the single biggest incentive in the game (permanent meta-progression) is hidden behind a defeat screen and an unlabeled shop-cart icon. The core loop is genuinely satisfying to watch (my tower visibly grew taller and stronger, a boss kill felt like a real milestone) and coin-star meta-progression carrying between runs is a strong "come back tomorrow" hook — but it's badly under-communicated. Structures crumbling with no visible cause and enemies that go completely unresponsive to taps for 10-20+ seconds were my two biggest frustration points and would have made me close the tab if I hadn't been specifically playtesting.

## Run 1 — what happened
- **~0-10s**: Game opened already mid-session on "Wave 1.1" with a "Monsters Incoming!" banner and a down-arrow pointing at one lane. Top bar showed three unlabeled numbers/icons (wood, puzzle-piece, coin-star) and four bottom buttons (VOLLEY + three cost-tagged cards). No goal statement, no control hint.
- **~5s**: Tapped a descending enemy directly — it died in one hit and dropped a puzzle-piece. This was the first (and only) way I learned tap-to-kill works — through experiment, not instruction.
- **~9-40s**: Discovered by dragging a bottom card onto a dashed slot near the base that it builds a turret (costs wood). Also discovered "REINFORCED HAND" (costs coin-star, rerolls the 3 cards) and "REPAIR ALL" (costs puzzle-pieces) purely by tapping them to see what happened.
- **~1 min**: Wave 3, daytime lighting change (nice touch, unexplained). Enemies started approaching from both left and right simultaneously — mechanic understood but never explicitly taught.
- **~1:20**: Successfully built a second tower slot; base visibly grew taller with two stacked cannons — this was the high point of "progress feels good."
- **~2:15 (wave 8)**: First boss fight ("CALL WAVE" turned into red "CALL BOSS" — good telegraphing). Boss took a long time to kill via taps; eventually died from tower fire. Big reward payout (+30 coin-star, puzzle-piece jumped to 100) — genuine milestone feeling.
- **~5:40 (wave 13)**: My entire tower structure began collapsing piece by piece across several waves with no clear cause (fire damage? enemy type? never shown). By wave 14 I had zero towers left, only the base door remained.
- **~5:41-6:20**: A lone armored enemy stood motionless near the wall and was completely unresponsive to 10+ direct taps and 20+ seconds of waiting — looked like a soft-lock. Tapping the never-before-used VOLLEY button (present since second 0, cooldown-gated) instantly killed it. This was the first time VOLLEY did anything for me, discovered by accident after 5.5 minutes.
- **~7:10 (wave 14-15)**: Same pattern repeated — two "stuck" wolf enemies froze the wave counter for 20+ seconds despite repeated taps; base kept losing structures with no repair-in-time possible.
- **~7:9 (430s)**: First "Destroyed!" screen — Wave 15 reached, Rank #308/361, "NEW RECORD!", +137 coin-star. A green "WAVE 14 +240 wood / +30 puzzle" button offered a one-time free continue — tapped it, resumed the SAME run instantly with no ad shown.
- **~7:53 (473s)**: Died again almost immediately after resuming (still wave 15, base was already mostly destroyed) — this time no continue offer appeared (confirms it's a one-shot perk). Final Run 1 result: Wave 15, Rank #308/361, +133 coin-star.

## Run 2 — what happened
Tapped the blue "skip" arrow from the Run 1 result screen, which started a fresh run from Wave 1 (different time-of-day skin — night — a nice bit of variety). Coin-star correctly carried over (421, confirming the permanent shop purchases persist). This time I played much more proactively: rebuilt both side towers immediately after every wave-held screen instead of waiting, and used VOLLEY on stuck/armored enemies as soon as it was off cooldown instead of hammering taps. Progress was visibly faster and towers survived longer through waves 1-7. However, at the Wave 8 boss fight I let a longer autoplay-wait run without checking in as often, and the boss overwhelmed my (still under-built) base — died at Wave 8, actually earlier than Run 1's Wave 15, despite better mechanical play. This shows the boss fight is a real difficulty spike that punishes inattention, and that "knowing the systems" doesn't automatically save you if you don't actively manage repairs during the fight. Result screen showed a new orange "3x [coin-star] 30" button (tripled the +47 reward instantly, no ad, one-time) alongside the same green continue offer, shop cart, and skip arrow. Total accumulated coin-star after both runs plus the 3x bonus: 658.

## Information gaps
1. **No onboarding/tutorial text at all.** What I saw: game opens directly into Wave 1 gameplay. What I didn't understand: the goal, controls, or any UI element. When: 0s. How long unclear: the core loop (tap-to-kill, drag-to-build) took ~10-40s of trial and error to infer; some systems (VOLLEY, coin-star's purpose) stayed unclear for 5.5-7+ minutes. Screenshot: `0001-shot.jpg`, `0002-wait.jpg`. Severity: **high**.
2. **VOLLEY button's function was never explained and looked disabled/greyed most of the time.** I didn't discover it does an instant AOE/finisher hit until 341s into the session, purely because I ran out of other options against a stuck enemy. Screenshot: `0227-tap.jpg`. Severity: **high** — this is a core combat tool for exactly the moments (stuck/armored enemies) where players are most frustrated, and it sat unused the whole time.
3. **Coin-star currency's purpose was undisclosed during 7+ minutes of active play**, despite being earned every wave. It's only explained via a tooltip inside the "Blueprints" permanent-upgrade shop, which itself is only reachable via an unlabeled shopping-cart icon on the post-defeat result screen. Screenshot: `0279-tap.jpg` / `0280-tap.jpg`. Severity: **high**.
4. **No visible overall base/tower health bar.** Only tiny colored slivers above individual placed pieces indicate health, and they're easy to miss in combat. I never knew how close to losing I was; structures disappeared between screenshots with no warning banner or damage-source callout. Screenshot: `0210-tap.jpg` through `0221-tap.jpg` (progressive tower destruction with no clear cause). Severity: **high**.
5. **"Stuck" enemies that don't respond to taps and don't advance.** Multiple times (an armored knight at wave 13, two wolves at wave 15, run 2 wave 7) an enemy would stand motionless near the wall, immune to repeated direct taps, freezing the wave counter for 10-20+ seconds. It was never clear whether this is intended (a melee unit that needs a different kill method) or a bug — no visual cue (shield icon, armor tint, "immune" text) distinguished it from a normal tappable enemy. Screenshots: `0224-tap.jpg`, `0272-tap.jpg`, `0274-tap.jpg`. Severity: **high**.
6. **Resource-cost transactions happen invisibly.** Wood is spent automatically for building without a confirmation step, and once I misjudged a tap as "attack an enemy" when it actually triggered a build purchase in an empty slot behind the enemy, spending wood I didn't intend to. Screenshot: `0013-tap.jpg`, `0056-tap.jpg`. Severity: **medium**.
7. **"REINFORCED HAND" and "HOARD +X%" labels were never explained.** I inferred REINFORCED HAND rerolls/upgrades the 3 build cards for coin-star, but the connection between "HOARD %" and anything I did was never clear. Screenshot: `0035-tap.jpg`. Severity: **medium**.
8. **Boss health bar was ambiguous** — a floating number (e.g. "11") appeared repeatedly near the boss and didn't visibly decrease, which read like "damage isn't working," when it was likely a per-hit damage number rather than remaining HP; only a thin orange bar above the boss was the real indicator. Screenshot: `0102-tap.jpg` through `0118-tap.jpg`. Severity: **medium**.

## Incentive gaps
1. **The single strongest incentive in the game (permanent Blueprints upgrade tree, paid in coin-star, explicitly "carries into every future run") is completely hidden until after a defeat**, and even then requires tapping an unlabeled cart icon. A commuter player who quits after one bad run (a very plausible outcome given gap #5 above) may never discover the entire meta-progression system that would make them want to return tomorrow. Screenshot: `0279-tap.jpg`. Severity: **high**.
2. **No pre-session or lobby screen at all** — the game drops you straight into a wave in progress with no menu, no daily reward, no streak counter, no "come back tomorrow for X" messaging. As a commuter who judges games by whether they give me a reason to return, I saw nothing structural that would pull me back beyond "my coin-star total is X now" — and I only even knew that total existed because I dug into the shop myself.
3. **Structure destruction feels punishing without clear cause or recovery path in the moment.** Watching my tower crumble piece by piece across waves 13-15 with no warning, no "your wall is about to break" alert, and no way to prioritize repairs before it happened was demotivating — progress (a tall, satisfying tower) can be undone in seconds for reasons I couldn't diagnose.
4. **The two "continue" mechanics (free wave-14/wave-7 resume, and the 3x coin-star multiplier) are excellent retention hooks but under-explained.** Neither showed a cost, a countdown, or copy explaining what tapping them does before I tapped — I only learned their effects after using them once. A first-time player might hesitate to tap an unlabeled button on a defeat screen, missing free value.
5. **What DID work well as an incentive**: the visible, snowballing tower growth (more/taller structures = visibly stronger) gave real moment-to-moment motivation; the wave counter and "CALL WAVE → CALL BOSS" color change created good anticipation; the boss-kill reward spike (puzzle-piece jumping from ~9 to 100) was a genuine "wow" moment; and "NEW RECORD!" + a global rank number (#308 of 361) on the result screen is a solid comparison hook.

## What worked (things that were clear or motivating)
- Tap-to-kill was intuitive once discovered (an enemy dies visibly and drops currency).
- Drag-card-to-empty-slot building, once discovered, was satisfying and visually clear (the tower physically grows).
- "CALL WAVE" countdown timer with an escalating "%" bonus number created a small sense of urgency/reward for waiting.
- Day/night lighting variety between waves and runs added visual freshness at no mechanical cost.
- "CALL BOSS" in red vs. "CALL WAVE" in blue was a genuinely clear telegraph of an upcoming harder fight.
- Global rank (#308 of 361) plus "NEW RECORD!" on the result screen is an effective, low-friction comparison hook.
- Coin-star correctly persisting across runs (confirmed 421 → 658 total) shows the meta-progression backend works correctly, even though its front-end communication is weak.

## Quit points
- **~5:40-7:00 (wave 13-15, Run 1)**: Watching my entire tower get destroyed piece by piece with no diagnosable cause, combined with 2-3 separate episodes of enemies frozen/unresponsive to tapping for 10-20+ seconds each, is exactly the kind of "the game feels broken and I can't tell why I'm losing" moment that would make a commuter close the tab. I only kept going because I was specifically instructed to reach a defeat.
- **Immediately after Run 1's first "Destroyed!" screen**, if the green continue button and shop hadn't been there, a player who felt the ending was confusing/unfair (structures crumbling with no clear reason) would have had zero reason shown to try again — the incentive (Blueprints shop) is there but invisible unless you go looking.
- **Run 2, wave 8 boss**: dying faster in Run 2 than Run 1 despite playing "better" (per my own understanding of the systems) would read to a real player as "the game is inconsistent/unfair," which is demotivating regardless of skill investment.

## Would you play a 3rd run? (1-5, why, and the single change that would raise it)
**3/5.** The core tap-and-build loop and the meta-progression shop are genuinely good hooks for my commuter play style — short bursts, visible growth, a reason to return via permanent upgrades. But the total absence of onboarding, the invisible-cause structure destruction, and the unresponsive/stuck-enemy episodes actively worked against continuing. The single change that would raise this to a 4-5: **surface the Blueprints/coin-star shop and explain VOLLEY during or immediately after Wave 1**, not hidden behind a defeat screen and an unlabeled icon — that's the strongest incentive in the game and it's currently invisible to anyone who doesn't dig for it or already lost once.
