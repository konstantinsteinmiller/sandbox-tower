# Playtester 2 — desktop, persona: strategy veteran

## Summary (5 lines max)
Tower Siege is a merge-tower-defense: place blocks on a wall/keep, merge identical weapons up tiers, survive escalating waves, spend a permanent "coins" currency in a Tech Tree between waves. Core loop (place → merge → upgrade → call wave) is teachable and I understood it within ~15 seconds thanks to a hands-on merge-diagram tutorial. Both runs died to the wave-10 tree boss, and both runs left me wanting a third — but three separate mechanics (passive-income buildings, Banners, weapon-fusion shapes) were explained ONLY on the post-death tip line, never during play when they'd actually help, and block placement has a real, repeatable UX bug where clicking silently fails or accidentally sells what you just built. The Tech Tree is the strongest retention hook in the game but its own color-coding (gold/blue/dim) wasn't explained until after Run 1 ended.

## Run 1 — what happened
- **0–2s**: Loading screen, then straight into "MONSTERS INCOMING", Wave 0, resource bar (wood/stone/coin), no goal text yet.
- **~5–13s**: Guided tutorial: select the "hand" tool → tap a wall slot → a merge-chain diagram appears (tile+tile→tier2 cannon→tier3→tier4) with "click to continue", no tutorial gate holdups. I placed my first block and the game auto-progressed a cannon through tiers, showing "-10%" then "-75%" damage popups comparing tiers.
- **~13s**: First real numbers panel appears when I tap the placed weapon: HP 120/120, DAMAGE 48, COOLDOWN 1.4s, RANGE 8.5, SPLASH 1.7, UPGRADE cost 24. I upgraded it immediately (exactly what a strategy player wants to do).
- **~15s**: Arrow points me to "CALL WAVE" — Wave 1 (3 enemies) begins, day/night cycle visible, pan/zoom hint shown, speed toggles (1x/1.5x/3x) appear.
- **~1 min**: Wave 1 held. Discovered the escalating "+N%" bonus timer on the Call Wave button (waiting = bigger reward, unexplained mechanic). Placed a Stone Block and a Wood Crate.
- **~1 min**: Wave 2 (7 enemies) held → a "SPEND!" badge revealed the Tech Tree (permanent meta-currency = coins). Bought Foundations (+6% block HP).
- **~1.5–3 min**: Waves 3–4 (16, 20 enemies), first flying enemies (bats) appear at night; unlocked and placed Archery ("Hits Fliers" — directly answers the flier threat, good clarity). Bought Sharpened Bolts (+7% dmg/rank).
- **~3.5 min**: Wave 5 held → Daily Missions panel discovered (3 quests already complete, +180 coins claimed in one big windfall). Spent heavily in the tree (Braced Crates unlock, Lumber Stockpile, more Sharpened Bolts, Rapid Fire).
- **~4–6.5 min**: Waves 6–9 held, each costing visible structure (blocks silently vanish after a wave with no damage report). Wave 9 held → button changes to red "CALL BOSS" (great escalation signal).
- **~7.5 min**: Wave 10 boss — a tree-monster with its own health bar walks in with regular mobs. Tower crumbled piece by piece; **DESTROYED**.
- **End of Run 1**: "YOU SURVIVED TO WAVE 10", New Record +264 coins, Best Score 159, global Rank #276 of 361. A tip appears for the first time: *"Lumber huts and stone pits pay you every wave."* — a building type I never saw or was offered in 10 full waves.

## Run 2 — what happened
Played faster/leaner this time since I understood the loop; used 3x speed between decisions.
- **0s**: New run resets the tower to a bare gate at Wave 0, but starting wood was 140 instead of 120 — tangible proof the Lumber Stockpile tech purchase carried over permanently, which felt great.
- **~1s**: Wave 1 auto-started with only a ~1s countdown — I had to scramble to place my first cannon mid-wave. Two Archery units placed side by side auto-merged into an upgraded double-bow, visibly demonstrating the "identical units fuse" rule (good, but only shown by accident of my own placement, not taught).
- **~1–4 min**: Waves 2–7 held while building and spending in the tech tree (Stone Stockpile, Braced Crates already owned, more Sharpened Bolts ranks).
- **~4.5 min**: Repeatedly hit a placement bug (see Information Gap #1) and, right before Wave 8, accidentally **sold a block I had just placed** by clicking near it again — resources fully refunded, block vanished, leaving the tower thin going into the wave.
- **~4.5 min**: Wave 8 — **DESTROYED**, worse than Run 1 (wave 8 vs wave 10), directly caused by the accidental-sell mistake. Score 90 this run vs best 159.
- **Death screen tip**: *"Banners boost every block they touch — and they multiply. Surround one gun instead of spreading them out."* — a Banner mechanic never seen or explained in either run (18 waves total).
- **New discovery**: a green "WAVE 7 +100 wood +100 stone" button for 30 coins **resumed the same run** from the last held wave with bonus resources, instead of starting over — a paid mid-run continue I only found by experimenting.
- **~5.5–8.5 min (continued run)**: Rebuilt with the bonus resources, held Waves 7–9, reached the boss again. Spent more tech (Rich Seams +14% producer yield).
- **~9.7 min**: Wave 10 boss — **DESTROYED** again, tying Run 1's ceiling exactly (wave 10), score 122.
- **Final tip**: *"Two identical weapons side by side fuse into one. Stack two of those to make a 2×2."* — the clearest statement of the merge rule in the whole game, and again it only appears after death.

## Information gaps
1. **Block placement silently fails on some grid cells.** What I saw: selecting an item and clicking a red-outlined preview cell; sometimes it commits instantly (resource deducted, block appears), sometimes the exact same click repeated many times never commits (resource count never changes) with no visual difference between the two states beforehand. First hit ~1.5 min into Run 1, recurred throughout both runs (at least 6 times), never resolved. Evidence: `0018-click.jpg`, `0019-click.jpg` (works) vs `0155-click.jpg`, `0203-click.jpg`, `0205-click.jpg` (stuck). Severity: **high** — it wastes real decisions right before waves start and I could never predict it.
2. **Clicking a placed block can accidentally sell it.** What I saw: after placing a Stone Block successfully (stone 140→108), clicking again nearby to place a second one instead reverted resources fully back to 140/140 and the first block vanished — an unintended sell, with zero confirmation dialog. First/clearest occurrence ~11.8 min game time (Run 2, right before Wave 8), directly caused that run's defeat. Evidence: `0205-click.jpg` → `0206-click.jpg`/`0207-click.jpg`. Severity: **high**.
3. **Passive-income buildings ("Lumber Huts", "Stone Pits") are never introduced during play.** First and only mention was the Run 1 death-screen tip; I never saw them offered in the build tray across 18 total waves. Evidence: `0133-click.jpg`. Severity: **high** — this is presented as a core economy mechanic ("change what you can afford") that a player would never discover through normal play.
4. **"Banners" mechanic is completely unexplained.** Only reference is a Run 2 death-screen tip about banners boosting/multiplying nearby blocks; no banner item ever appeared in my tray or tech tree in either run. Evidence: `0244-wait.jpg`'s predecessor, the Run 2 defeat screen. Severity: **medium-high**.
5. **Tech Tree color coding (gold/blue/dim) and permanence were unexplained until after Run 1 ended.** I opened and used the Tech Tree for ~7 minutes across Run 1 without knowing whether purchases carried between runs, or what node colors meant beyond guessing "gold = affordable." The explanatory tooltips only appeared when I clicked the post-death "cart" icon. Evidence: `0134-click.jpg`, `0135-click.jpg`, `0136-click.jpg`. Severity: **high** — this is the game's main progression system and its rules arrive too late to inform in-run decisions.
6. **Unit/range/damage numbers have no units.** The weapon panel shows "RANGE 8.5" and "SPLASH 1.7" with no unit label (tiles? meters?) and no way to compare against the actual enemy approach distance on screen. Seen from ~13s onward, stayed unclear the whole session. Evidence: `0011-click.jpg`. Severity: **low-medium** for a strategy player who wants to optimize range coverage.
7. **Destroyed blocks give no feedback.** Structures visibly disappear between waves (e.g., a base-door crate, a roofed house, wood crates) with no damage summary, no "you lost X block" notice, and no indicator of why one block died and an adjacent one survived. First noticed ~5.5 min into Run 1. Severity: **medium**.
8. **The escalating "Call Wave" bonus percentage (+8%, +31%, etc.) is never explained.** I could see the number grow the longer I waited before calling the next wave, but no tooltip ever said what it applies to (extra coins? resources? both?). Seen from Wave 1 onward in both runs. Severity: **low-medium**.
9. **A wavy dark shape periodically appears in the water** (bottom-right of screen) suggesting an aquatic threat, and a later Tech Tree node ("Harbour — moor ships that alone can hit submerged foes") confirms aquatic enemies exist, but I never saw one attack or understood the threat during actual gameplay. Evidence: `0062-wait.jpg` (wavy shape), `0140-click.jpg` (Harbour node). Severity: **medium**.

## Incentive gaps
1. **The single biggest incentive — the permanent Tech Tree — is nearly invisible.** It's revealed only by a small "SPEND!" callout over a tiny green diamond icon after Wave 2; a player who misses that callout (easy on a first playthrough busy watching a wave) could finish a whole run never knowing permanent progression exists. Severity: **high**.
2. **Daily Missions were also nearly missed** — signposted only by a small red badge on a small clipboard icon in the bottom-left corner, easy to overlook amid the wave-1/wave-2 action. When I did open it, all three quests were already complete and paid out a large coin windfall (+180) with a satisfying animation — this was one of the best moments in the whole session, but it happened by luck of me clicking around, not by design guiding me there. Severity: **medium-high** (great payoff, poor discoverability).
3. **No comparison shown before spending an Upgrade or Tech Tree purchase.** I could see current stats and cost, but never a preview of the resulting stats, so I couldn't judge whether a 24-coin upgrade or a 60-coin unlock was the better buy — I was buying blind and just trusting "more is better." Severity: **medium**.
4. **Post-defeat tips consistently describe mechanics I never got a chance to use** (Lumber Huts/Stone Pits, Banners, weapon-fusion shapes) rather than mechanics I actually touched. This makes the game feel like it's always one system ahead of what it taught me, which is frustrating rather than motivating — it reads as "you lost because you didn't know a thing we never told you," not "here's how to do better." Severity: **high**.
5. **The paid mid-run continue (+100/+100 resources for 30 coins) is a genuinely strong retention hook** — it let me instantly get back to the boss fight instead of grinding from Wave 0 — but it appeared with zero explanation of what it does before I clicked it, and was greyed out the first time I saw it for reasons I never understood. Severity: **medium** (good idea, unclear execution).
6. **Score, Best Score, and Rank felt disconnected from what I actually experienced.** "Best Score 159" persisted across both defeats without changing (Run 2's two segments scored 90 and 122, both under 159), and I was never told what "Score" measures (wave reached? resources banked? something else?). The leaderboard rank (#276 of 361) is a nice competitive hook for my persona, but with an unexplained scoring formula it's hard to know what to optimize. Severity: **medium**.
7. **Peak motivation was Wave 5→6 of Run 1** (Daily Mission windfall + first big Tech Tree shopping spree) and it **dropped hard right after both defeats** at the boss — not because the boss felt unfair, but because both times my structure had silently eroded in the waves leading up to it with no way to see it coming or reinforce specifically the parts that were weak.

## What worked
- The opening tutorial (merge-diagram → live demo of "-10%" vs "-75%" damage) taught the core verb (place → merge → upgrade) in under 15 seconds without ever blocking me with a modal I had to dismiss to keep playing.
- The weapon info panel (HP/DAMAGE/COOLDOWN/RANGE/SPLASH + Upgrade/Sell) is exactly the granular data a strategy player wants, and it appeared very early (~13s).
- "CALL WAVE" changing to red "CALL BOSS" was a clean, wordless escalation signal that correctly primed me to prepare.
- Enemy variety (regular mobs, flying bats explicitly countered by Archery's stated "Hits Fliers," and a boss with its own health bar) gave visible reasons to diversify my build.
- The permanent-progression payoff was demonstrated concretely: Run 2 started with 140 wood instead of 120 because of a Run 1 purchase — the single clearest "your time was not wasted" moment in the whole session.

## Quit points
- **~4.5 min into Run 2** (right after the accidental-sell bug wiped a block I'd just placed and the very next wave destroyed my tower): this is the moment a player who didn't understand *why* they lost would plausibly close the tab, since the loss traces to a UI mistake, not a strategic one.
- **Immediately after either boss defeat**, if the death-screen tip hadn't referenced something new and slightly enticing each time ("lumber huts," "banners," "weapon fusion"), I would have had no reason to load a third run — the tip line is functionally the game's only re-engagement pitch at that moment.

## Would you play a 3rd run? (1–5, why, and the single change that would raise it)
**3/5.** The merge-and-defend loop and the Tech Tree are compelling enough that I'd try again to see the Banner and Lumber Hut mechanics the death screen kept teasing — that curiosity is doing real work. But losing a run to a UI misclick (accidental sell) rather than a strategic misjudgment is the kind of thing that would make me stop trusting my own inputs, and for a numbers-focused player, not knowing what "Score" means or what the Call-Wave bonus percentage actually rewards keeps me from feeling like I'm optimizing anything.
**Single change that would raise it to a 5:** surface the mechanics currently hidden in death-screen tips (Lumber Huts/Stone Pits, Banners, weapon-fusion shapes) during actual play — e.g., as an unlock notification or a tray item the moment they become available — and fix/confirm block placement so a click either visibly places or visibly does nothing, never silently sells.
