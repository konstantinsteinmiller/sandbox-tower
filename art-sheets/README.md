# Tower Siege — art contact sheets

Generated by the `/art-sheets` bench (`src/views/ArtSheets.vue`, dev only).
**Do not hand-edit anything in this folder** — re-run the bench instead.

Tower Siege ships with zero gameplay bitmaps: every block, enemy and engine is
drawn procedurally in `src/use/useTowerArt.ts`. These sheets are that cast baked
out onto a fixed pixel lattice, so it can be handed to an image model, repainted,
and sliced back in.

---

## The lattice is the contract

Every cell is **exactly 128 × 128 px** and sits at an exact multiple of 128 from
the sheet's top-left. No gutters, no padding, no centring fudge. Slicing is
integer arithmetic, and `sheet-index.json` says which slice belongs where.

Because of that, **nothing is written inside a cell**. Captions live on the
separate `-key` sheet. Text inside a frame is text an image model will
faithfully repaint as art.

The clean sheets have a **transparent** ground, because the renderer composites
this art with alpha.

**Ask the model for magenta, though, not transparency.** Requesting a
transparent background is unreliable — one attempt came back fully opaque, and
it painted the grey-and-white transparency checkerboard in as if it were art. So
the generated prompts ask for a solid `#FF00FF` ground, and the slicer keys it
out on the way back in. Its test (green under 70 with red and blue both over
190) matches nothing in `PALETTES` — the closest is the bat's `#ff5ad0`, clear
by twenty points of green — so it is always safe to run.

## Three ways in

**Single cells — use these.** `cell-<id>.png`, one object per image at 512 px,
with a prompt each in `PROMPTS-CELLS.md`. 46 of them, one per asset that has
somewhere to go.

Three passes through an image model lost the grid three different ways: a whole
sheet drifted 4 px a cell and matted objects onto cards; a strip came back at
half size and 4% squashed; the next strip **invented a ninth object in an
eight-cell row** and crammed the remaining six into five squares. Not one of
those passes lost the *art* — by the third the style was right. What a model
will not do is hold a lattice while it repaints, because repainting is a
re-composition and the grid is not something it has a reason to preserve.

So do not ask it to. One object, one image, nothing to drift, nothing to
miscount, and the model's whole resolution budget goes to one asset instead of
an eighth of it. More generations, but every one usable.

**Row strips.** `strip-<sheet>-r<N>.png`, one row at 256 px a cell, prompts in
`PROMPTS-STRIPS.md`. Good for a fast style test across eight related things at
once — just expect to check the alignment.

**Whole sheets.** `sheet-<name>.png`. An overview, and the quickest way to judge
a style direction across the whole cast.

## Blocks share bases

Twenty block types read from **seven** bitmaps. Almost every block is a slab of
material with a machine bolted on, and the machine is drawn live — the bow, the
barrel, the coil, the blade and the pick are all `drawFixture`, every frame. So
the bitmap only has to say what the block is MADE OF, and the fixture on top
carries the identity.

| Base | Worn by |
|---|---|
| `timber` | wood, archer, repair, sawmill, lumberHut, spikes |
| `masonry` | stone, cannon, quarry, stonepit, mint |
| `metal` | mortar, bombard, tesla, frost, coffer |
| `braced` | brace |
| `arcane` | obelisk |
| `heraldry` | banner |
| `gate` | gate |

The last four keep their own art because nothing is drawn over them to tell them
apart — the Gate's door, the braced crate's X and the obelisk's glyphs *are* the
block.

The map is `BLOCK_ART_BASE` in `src/game/art.ts`, applied inside `spriteFor`
rather than at the call sites: five places paint a block face (world, build
ghost, water reflection, tray tile, shape preview) and a mapping applied per
site is one that eventually gets missed at a site — which is exactly the bug the
build ghost already had.

On the sheets, only the FIRST block of each base carries a delivery target;
the rest are marked `uses <base> base` and are reference. A base's prompt
describes the material and nothing block-specific, because five different
machines get drawn on top of it.

## Reading the blocks sheet

Three rows per band of eight, and the order matters:

1. **assembled** — the block as the player sees it, gun and all. Reference.
2. **body** — the block *without* its moving part. **This is the row you slice.**
3. **tray icon** — the build-tray tile: body, dark backing disc, pale pictogram.

> **The single biggest trap.** For every weapon and economy block, the barrel,
> bow, coil, blade and wrench are composited **live, every frame**, on top of
> whatever `public/images/blocks/<id>.webp` decodes to. Slice the *assembled*
> row into the game and the block grows a second barrel. Slice the *body* row.
>
> To bake a whole block into one bitmap instead, delete that block's `case` in
> `drawFixture` (`src/use/useTowerArt.ts`).

## Cells that have no home yet

Painting these is not wasted — they just need a small renderer change first.

| Cells | Why | What it would take |
|---|---|---|
| All 27 merged cells | `drawBlock` reads one bitmap per block **id**, for every tier | Key the probe by `(id, tier)` |
| `skiff` / `longship` / `galley` | Ships branch out before the block-sprite path — the hull is drawn, clipped at the waterline and rolled every frame | A probe inside the ship branch |
| Projectiles | No `projectile` probe exists | Add one keyed by `kind` |
| Sun, moon, trees | The background is fully procedural and re-tinted per wave tier | A `bg` probe keyed by tier |
| Tray icons | Drawn from `blockGlyph.ts` paths, not bitmaps | A separate icon probe |

Not on any sheet: the **mountains, forest band, grass and water**. They are
full-width parallax layers that tile horizontally — a 128 px square would give
you a picture of a mountain rather than a tileable range. They want a wide strip
sheet, which is a different job.

Also absent: the **`zap` round**. Lightning is a polyline rebuilt every frame
from the simulation's own fork points, so there is nothing standalone to draw,
and a blank cell is an invitation to invent one.

## Enemies are animated — think before overriding one

Blocks are the natural fit for this pipeline: a block never moves, so one
bitmap is the whole truth about it. **Enemies are not.**

Every enemy body is a monster design from `monsters.ts`, baked by
`monsterSprites.ts` into a 16-frame strip covering exactly one locomotion
cycle — 17 designs, 272 frames — and the battlefield plays that strip. Siege
engines go further: their wheels turn with distance travelled, so a halted
engine's wheels stop, which is the player's only tell that it has set up to
fire.

`drawEnemy` early-returns on an override and blits the still. So a repainted
enemy does not restyle the character, it **freezes** it: a static pose sliding
across the ground, with the walk, the wing-beat and the wheel-turn gone.

The slicer warns when you write to `images/enemies/` or `images/allies/`, but it
still lets you, because a strong enough still may be worth it for a boss.

If you want hand-drawn enemies that still move, the honest routes are:

1. **Restyle the procedural designs** — edit the `draw` functions in
   `monsters.ts` toward the new look. Keeps all 16 frames and every animation.
2. **Extend `drawEnemy` to blit a sprite strip** and supply 16 consistent
   frames per design. Image models cannot currently hold a grid of eight cells,
   let alone a coherent 16-frame cycle, so this needs a real animator.

## Rules a repaint must not break

* **Enemies face RIGHT.** The battlefield mirrors by travel direction. A
  left-facing repaint walks backwards into the tower. (Designs authored facing
  left were already flipped for you on the sheet.)
* **Sea creatures are fish, not serpents** — deep body, sweeping caudal fin,
  dorsal and pectoral fins, gill slit, one round eye. They are drawn twice per
  frame and clipped at the waterline, so the tail must trail **downward**.
* **The Ironclad Ram is cold iron.** It is the only enemy immune to arrows, and
  the riveted plate with spent arrows lodged in it is the entire way the player
  is told. Never paint it as timber.
* **The cavalry is the friendly unit** — the coolest, brightest thing on the
  field. A friendly that reads as a target is worse than no art.
* **Palette identity is readability.** The source of truth is `PALETTES` in
  `src/game/art.ts`. `debris` there is the RGB the particle system throws when
  the thing breaks, so matching it makes destruction VFX agree with new art for
  free.
* **Blocks must be stackable.** This is a block-over-block builder: the player
  puts another block directly on top of and beside each one. So a block is a
  square with a FLAT top, flat bottom and flat sides, filling its frame edge to
  edge. No battlements, wheels, peaked roofs, rounded shoulders or legs —
  anything protruding above the top edge leaves the block above it floating, and
  any margin becomes a visible seam in the middle of a wall. A weapon mount is a
  fitting **on the face** of the cube, never the block's outline.

  This is what the third pass got wrong: it returned the archery post as a
  crenellated turret and the cannon as a wheeled cart. Both good art, neither
  stackable. The prompts now carry it as a hard rule.

## Getting art back into the game

**1 · Drop the repainted images in `art-sheets/painted/`** — strips or whole
sheets, mixed freely. Any filename that still contains the target's name works:
`strip-blocks-r1.png`, `strip-blocks-r1-v3.png`, `sheet-enemies.png`. The slicer
matches the longest name it recognises, so a strip is never mistaken for its
parent sheet.

**2 · Slice them:**

```
pnpm slice-sheets              # everything in art-sheets/painted/
pnpm slice-sheets --dry        # print the plan, write nothing
pnpm slice-sheets art-sheets/painted/sheet-blocks.png
pnpm slice-sheets --only icon-chest    # that one file; nothing else is rewritten
```

**Adding ONE asset to a set that is already compressed? Use `--only`.** A sheet
is sliced whole by default, so a run that exists to pick up a single new bitmap
re-encodes every other target on that sheet as well — the art looks identical
and the compressed copies are gone. `--only` narrows the run to what is
actually being added: pass a cell id (`icon-chest`, `ui/coin`,
`block/gate/body`), a target path (`images/icons/chest.webp`) or just its file
name (`chest`). It repeats, it takes comma-separated lists, a walk cycle is
always selected as a whole strip, and matching is exact — a selector that hits
nothing fails the run rather than quietly writing everything.

Each sliceable cell is cut, encoded as WebP and written to its `target` under
`public/`. A cell keeps its painted size (at most 512) — slice the `@2x` sheet
and blocks land at 256 × 256, which is what `art-todo.md` recommends. **Walk
strips and single-image sheets (monsters, engines, arms, hulls, fixtures) are
written at most 256 px tall** — the payload rule every shipped strip already
follows. The fixtures on disk today are 640 × 640 and the siege chassis
360 × 360, so a re-slice brings them down to 256; `--size <px>` is the one-run
exception if one of them turns out soft on screen.

Useful flags: `--sheet <id>` if the filename is unrecognisable, `--size <px>` to
force the output edge, `--out <dir>` to write somewhere other than `public/`,
`--quality 0-1` for the WebP encoder, `--no-fit` when the DRAWING is the thing
that is off (a part tiny in its own cell) and registering onto it would shrink a
good painting.

**The receipt.** Every painting that is cut leaves a line in
`painted/.sliced.json`: the revision of the reference it was cut against, and
the painting's own hash. When the bench re-draws a reference after it was
painted, the slicer **refuses** that painting — slicing it would put the old
silhouette back over the corrected drawing. Repaint it, or pass `--stale-ok` if
the change was cosmetic. A painting whose drawing was re-cut goes to
`painted/stale/` (never deleted — delete its sliced `.webp` files instead, so
the renderer falls back to the corrected drawing).

**Set your image tool's aspect ratio to 1:1.** Two separate single-cell returns
came back at exactly 1024x559 — that is a tool default overriding the prompt,
and no wording beats a setting. When it does drift, `--fit squash` (the default)
keeps every feature and distorts slightly; `--fit crop` keeps proportions and
loses the outer edges. On a cannon block, squash kept both iron straps and crop
cut them off, so squash is the default for a reason.

What the slicer does for you:

* **Keys out the magenta ground** and de-fringes the anti-aliased rim, so
  sprites do not wear a pink halo. `--no-chroma` turns it off.
* **Corrects proportion drift on a strip.** Models routinely return
  1024 × 123 where 2048 × 256 was asked for. A strip is one row, so a uniform
  vertical squash is recoverable — the cells still divide the width evenly and
  every one is distorted identically, so each is stretched back to square. Up to
  15%; beyond that it refuses.
* **Refuses a multi-row SHEET whose aspect changed.** There a vertical mismatch
  means the rows no longer land where the index says and every rect is wrong. It
  exits non-zero and writes nothing rather than producing 40 subtly misaligned
  sprites.
* **Restores the letterboxed UI bitmaps** to their real shape — the ribbon goes
  back to 553 × 188, not a padded square.
* **Checks that every block reaches its own edges.** A block is stacked against
  its neighbours, so rounded corners or a polite margin put a visible seam on
  every side. Any block sprite whose edges are under 90% covered gets a warning
  naming the edges that fall short.
* **Squares up a single-cell image** whatever shape it arrives in — see the
  aspect note above.

**2b · Compress what it wrote.** The slicer encodes WebP at q0.92, well above
what the art needs. `pnpm compress:images` runs `scripts/compress-images.mjs`
over `public/images`, searching each file for the smallest encode that still
clears measured quality floors, and keeps every original under `public-backup/`
(never inside `public/` — Vite ships whatever is in there). After a re-slice,
compress only the files the slicer just rewrote, from themselves:

```
node scripts/compress-images.mjs public/images --backup-dir public-backup --fresh \
     --only public/images/monsters/grumpling.webp
```

Without `--fresh` a re-cut file that already has a backup is skipped (shipped
uncompressed), and `--force` would compress the OLD backup back over the new
art. The Art Desk does this for you on every slice. `pnpm compress-folder <dir>
--dry-run` prints the plan and scores without writing; `pnpm compress-bench`
re-derives the settings, and `docs/IMAGE-COMPRESSION-BENCH.md` has the numbers
behind the defaults.

**2c · Check it landed.** `pnpm art:status` lists, per folder, which drop-ins are
on disk and which are still drawn — a miss is silent in the game by design —
plus files that ship without any manifest target pointing at them.
`pnpm art:measure cells` measures every sliced strip against the box the bench
recorded for its drawing (oversize, undersize, off-centre, fixtures judged by
area as the slicer fits them); `pnpm art:measure strips public/images/monsters`
finds walk frames the key ate. Then look at `/#/playground` with the art flag.

**3 · Turn the art on with the feature flag:**

| | |
|---|---|
| `?art=on` | Use the bitmaps. **Remembered**, so the param can be dropped afterwards. |
| `?art=off` | Back to the procedural art. Also remembered. |
| `__art.on()` / `__art.off()` | Same, live from the console — no reload. |
| `__art.refresh()` | Re-read the files from disk after re-slicing, without a reload. |
| `__art.status()` | `{ enabled, source, probes }` — `source` is `url`, `stored` or `build`. |

Flip it off the moment the new art looks worse than the drawn version; the
procedural path is always still there.

**4 · To ship it enabled**, set `VITE_ENABLE_ART_OVERRIDES=true` in `.env`. That
is the build default and the only layer a portal ever sees — **it is currently
empty, so a portal build probes nothing at all.** That is deliberate: with no
override art shipping, every probe missed, and CrazyGames' QA console reports
each miss as `Missing resource detected: …/images/blocks/wood.webp`, one line
per block and per enemy, which reads as a broken build to a reviewer.

A miss is never an error — the procedural path just keeps drawing — so art can
be adopted one file at a time.

> **Why the flag has three layers.** URL param beats remembered value beats
> build default. The build default has to stay the floor so a portal never sees
> a 404 storm, while a URL param still lets art be switched on for a look and
> straight back off, live, with no rebuild.

## Art that does not exist yet — merged tiers and the chest wing

Two kinds of slot are for paintings nobody has made yet, so the game does
**not** probe them until they are listed — a probe for a file that is not there
is a 404, and a portal's QA console reads each one as a broken build.

* **Merged weapons** — `fixture-<weapon>-t2.png` and `-t3.png` for cannon,
  archer, bombard, mortar, tesla and frost (prompts in `PROMPTS-WALKS.md`).
  The reference is the procedural twin / triple battery the game shows today;
  attach the plain painting (`public/images/fixtures/<weapon>.webp`) as well,
  because it is the design. Returns land at
  `public/images/fixtures/<weapon>-t2.webp` / `-t3.webp`; tiers 4 and up reuse
  the t3 painting. The game lays the tier rim (lighter) and the tier-3 glow on
  top at run time, so neither belongs in the painting.
* **The chest wing** — `prop-chest-wing` (prompt in `PROMPTS-SCENERY.md`, no
  reference image: attach `public/images/icons/chest.webp`). One RIGHT wing on
  a 512 × 512 canvas, root at 12 % from the left and 70 % from the top — the
  component flaps it by rotating about that point, so the prompt pins it.
  Lands at `public/images/props/chest-wing.webp`.

After slicing, **list the id** in `src/game/paintedArt.ts` —
`PAINTED_TIER_FIXTURES` (`'cannon-t2'`) or `PAINTED_PROPS` (`'chest-wing'`).
`tests/game/paintedArt.test.ts` fails until you do, and fails again if an id is
listed with no file behind it.

## The Art Desk — the loop without the busywork

```
pnpm art:desk
```

A local page (127.0.0.1 only) over the same files: search any sheet by sprite
name, **Copy image, then prompt** (key <kbd>b</kbd>) and paste twice at Gemini,
press Gemini's download button — the desk files the download from `~/Downloads`
under the right name in `painted/`, slices it and compresses exactly what was
cut. Dropping or pasting an image onto a job does the same. Its status column is
`PAINT-STATUS.md`, recomputed live; reference-only strips are marked `–` and
never queued.

Or queue every **To paint** job and let it drive gemini.google.com in its own
Chrome window (`~/.art-desk/gemini-chrome`, shared by every project), 90–150 s
apart and capped at 40 a day. Sign in once with **Sign in** — Google refuses a
sign-in in a window with a debugging port, so that one opens plain; close it
afterwards. A painting it replaces goes to `painted/replaced/` (git-ignored),
never deleted. Settings go in an optional `art-desk.config.json` at the repo root
(see the header of `tools/art-desk/config.mjs`).

## Regenerating

**Only the prompt text changed** (a blurb, a clause, a new manifest entry):

```
pnpm art:prompts            # the four PROMPTS-*.md + PAINT-STATUS.md, no browser
pnpm art:prompts --check    # exit 1 if a document is out of date
```

Both routes render through `promptDocs()` in `src/game/artSheet.ts`, so they
write the same bytes, and `tests/game/artPromptDocs.test.ts` proves every block
parses back out exactly as the desk will send it.

**The drawing changed** — re-export the references:

```
pnpm dev
open http://localhost:2050/#/art-sheets
click "Export all sheets"          # or, with the dev server up: pnpm art:export
```

Every animated thing is frozen at a fixed phase, so a re-export is
byte-identical unless the art actually changed — a diff here means something in
the renderer moved.

The route is dev-only (`import.meta.env.DEV` in `src/router/index.ts`) and the
write endpoint is `apply: 'serve'`, so neither exists in any portal build.
